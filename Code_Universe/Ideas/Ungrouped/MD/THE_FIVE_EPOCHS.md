## THE PATTERN

Before the strategy. Before the pitch. The pattern.

Every major transition in the music business has followed the same arc:

1. A new technology emerges that changes how sound can be captured, transmitted, or reproduced
2. A new power class emerges that controls access to that technology
3. The creators produce the work
4. The power class extracts the value
5. The creators receive a fraction
6. The next technology disrupts the power class
7. A new power class takes over
8. Repeat

**NOIZY breaks the pattern.** Not by winning within it — by building infrastructure that makes the extraction mechanism structurally impossible.

The history is the evidence. The fifth epoch is the correction.

## EPOCH I — THE NOTATION AGE (1400s–1920s)
### *The Printer Holds the Power*

**Primary Product:** Sheet Music — physical notation on paper
**Value Mechanism:** Printing monopoly. First Rights. Physical sale of paper.
**Who Held the Power:** The printer. Ottaviano Petrucci secured the first music printing monopoly in 1498, establishing the original template for music rights: whoever controls reproduction controls the income.
**What the Creator Got:** Their name on the sheet music. A fraction of print sales. The performance income, if they could get the gig.
**How It Ended:** The phonograph. Thomas Edison and Emile Berliner invented a way to capture sound itself — not its notation. The printer's monopoly on reproduction became irrelevant overnight. A new power class stepped in.

**What EPOCH I taught us:** The first person to control the reproduction technology controls the money. The creator is secondary to the machine that copies their work.

## EPOCH II — THE RECORDING ERA (1920s–1990s)
### *The Label Holds the Power*

**Primary Product:** The Recording — a physical object containing a performance
**Value Mechanism:** Physical sale of an object. One-time purchase. Manufacturing, distribution, retail.
**Who Held the Power:** The record label. They owned the studios, the pressing plants, the distribution networks, the retail relationships. An artist without a label could not reach an audience. An artist with a label signed over their master recordings in exchange for access.
**What the Creator Got:** A royalty rate of 8–15% of wholesale after recoupment. Recoupment meant the label deducted recording costs, packaging costs, breakage allowances (yes, from the 1940s, still applied in the 1980s), and "promotional" costs from the artist's royalty account. Most artists never recouped. Most artists made almost nothing.
**How It Ended:** The digital file. Napster. The MP3. A recording could now be copied and distributed at zero cost. The physical object lost its value. The label lost its stranglehold on distribution. A new power class stepped in — and learned nothing from what had just happened.

**What EPOCH II taught us:** The creator's voice, once captured, becomes the label's asset. The creator is licensed to perform in exchange for a fraction of what their performance is worth.

## EPOCH III — THE DIGITAL DISRUPTION (2000s)
### *The Platform Holds the Power Briefly*

**Primary Product:** The File — an MP3, a digital download, a ripped CD
**Value Mechanism:** File ownership. $0.99 per song. Or piracy — free.
**Who Held the Power:** Briefly, nobody. Then Apple, with iTunes, established the $0.99 download as the digital standard. Labels received approximately $0.70 per download and passed approximately $0.09 to artists (after recoupment).
**What the Creator Got:** $0.09 per download if signed. $0.70 per download if they sold direct — but they had no distribution, no discovery, and no way to reach the audience that iTunes had.
**How It Ended:** Streaming. Why buy a file when you can access everything for $10/month? The download collapsed. Piracy receded. A new power class — the streaming platform — stepped in with a model that was even more extractive than anything before it.

**What EPOCH III taught us:** Digital distribution should have empowered creators directly. Instead, it created a new intermediary (iTunes) that took its cut and returned the creator to the same fraction-of-a-fraction position they had always occupied.

## EPOCH IV — THE STREAMING AGE (2010s–Present)
### *The Algorithm Holds the Power*

**Primary Product:** Access — a subscription that permits streaming of licensed recordings
**Value Mechanism:** Recurring micro-payments. $0.003–$0.005 per stream. Platforms pay per-stream royalties from a pool. The pool is divided by pro-rata share of total streams. Artists who are not already famous receive almost nothing.
**Who Holds the Power:** The streaming platform. They control the algorithm. The algorithm controls discovery. Discovery controls streams. Streams control income. The creator produces content for the algorithm. The algorithm decides which content reaches which people. The creator has no say in the algorithm.
**What the Creator Gets:** $0.003 per stream. To earn $30,000/year — below minimum wage in most Western cities — a creator needs 10 million streams. The platform that hosts their work is worth hundreds of billions of dollars. The creator cannot negotiate. The terms are take-it-or-leave-it.
**The New Extraction:** AI training. Every recording ever uploaded to a streaming platform has been ingested into AI training datasets without artist consent. The platforms have argued that this is permitted under their terms of service. The art became the raw material for a technology that now competes with the artist. The creator produced the training data for their own replacement.
**How It Will End:** The same way every epoch has ended — a new technology that disrupts the power class. But this time, the disruption is being built *by the creators*, not by a new intermediary. For the first time in the five-epoch history of the music business, the transition is being led by the people who were always at the bottom of every prior era.

**What EPOCH IV teaches us:** The platform is not neutral. The algorithm is not neutral. The terms of service are not a contract between equals. The creator is the product, not the customer. And the latest version of the extraction — AI training — is the most complete form of creator cannibalization in the 600-year history of the music business.
