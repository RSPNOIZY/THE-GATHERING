## THE MECHANISM

Here is exactly how the cannibal works.

**Step 1 — Ingest.**
The platform takes the music. The voice. The sound. The forty years of practice, the 3am sessions, the cultural inheritance, the fingerprints on the frequency. It takes all of it. No permission asked. Terms of service updated at 2am on a Tuesday. By continuing to use the platform you agree.

**Step 2 — Digest.**
The model trains on what it ate. It learns the patterns — the emotional arc of a blues progression, the breath between the words of a Griot, the way a voice cracks on the note that costs something to reach. It learns all of it. It does not credit what it learned from. It does not remember where the knowledge came from. The meal disappears into the machine.

**Step 3 — Replicate.**
The platform now generates. Instantly. Infinitely. Content that sounds like the artists it ate, costs nothing to produce, and competes directly with the artists it ate for the same algorithmic real estate on the same platform that ate them.

**Step 4 — Displace.**
The algorithm rewards the generated content. It's faster. It's cheaper. It fits the format perfectly because it was trained on the format. The human artists — the ones who created the original signal — get fewer plays. Less discovery. Lower royalties. The platform calls this "the market."

**Step 5 — Pay Insults.**
$0.003 per stream. Eighteen months later. Through three intermediaries. After the label takes its cut. After the distributor takes its cut. After the platform takes its 30%. What arrives in the artist's account is a number so small it cannot be described as payment without insulting the word.

**Step 6 — Deny Responsibility.**
The platform issues a statement about its commitment to artists. It announces a new program. A fund. A portal. An initiative with a name that contains the word "creator." It does not change the math. It does not change the consent architecture. It does not stop eating.

**Step 7 — Go Back to Step 1.**

## WHAT IT FORGETS

The cannibal eats without memory.

It does not remember that every song it ingested was someone's emergency. Someone's survival. Someone's only way of saying the thing they couldn't say any other way.

It does not remember the session musician who played on forty albums and can now barely pay rent because the music she helped make is in a model that generates her sound for free.

It does not remember the voice actor who spent thirty years building a vocal instrument of extraordinary precision and expressiveness, who woke up one morning to find her voice style in a product she never consented to.

It does not remember the Griot whose oral tradition contains knowledge that has no other container — not a book, not a database, not a transcript — only the living voice, the living memory, the living performance. Ingested. Digested. Compressed into a feature vector. The civilization reduced to a training signal.

It does not remember the composer who wrote the theme that became the sonic identity of a generation — who gets no credit, no royalty, no acknowledgment when the model that learned his harmonic language generates a hundred thousand songs in his style per day.

**The cannibal forgets because forgetting is efficient.**

Memory is expensive. Attribution is expensive. Consent is expensive. Payment is expensive.

Forgetting is free.

## WHY IT IS CANNIBALISTIC — SPECIFICALLY

Cannibalism is not just eating. It is eating your own kind.

The platform needs creators. It was built on creativity. Its entire value proposition — the reason anyone pays for a subscription, the reason advertisers pay for placement, the reason investors value it at billions — is the creative work of human beings.

It eats the thing it depends on to survive.

And every meal makes the next meal harder to find — because every artist it displaces is an artist who produces less, who stops, who finds another way to live, who takes their cultural knowledge and their sonic fingerprint and their forty years with them when they go.

The cannibal is eating its own future.

It does not see this. Or it sees it and calculates that the damage arrives after the next earnings call.

Either way, the artists pay the price.
