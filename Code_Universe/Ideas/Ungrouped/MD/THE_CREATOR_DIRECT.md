## THE UNION QUESTION

Rob has stated clearly: he would prefer to bypass the unions and give everything directly to the creators.

This deserves an honest analysis.

### What Unions Have Done Well

Music and entertainment unions — SAG-AFTRA, the AFM, Equity, the Writers Guild, and their international equivalents — have done real things for real people:

- Negotiated minimum rates that prevented complete race-to-the-bottom pricing in certain sectors
- Provided health insurance to working artists who would otherwise have none
- Created pension funds that provide retirement income for session musicians and voice actors
- Won legislative battles around digital performance rights that generated hundreds of millions in artist income
- Most recently, SAG-AFTRA's AI provisions in the 2023 agreement were the first major union win specifically protecting members from unconsented AI voice replication

**These are real. They should be acknowledged.**

### What Unions Cannot Do

But unions have structural limitations that make them the wrong vehicle for what NOIZY is building:

**Geographic walls.** The AFM and SAG-AFTRA protect their members in their jurisdictions. A Griot musician in Senegal, a throat singer in Tuva, a gamelan player in Bali — these artists have no union. They have no protection. Their work is the most at-risk of extraction and the least protected by any existing structure. NOIZY's consent architecture protects them from day one.

**Negotiation is reactive, not preventive.** Unions negotiate with companies after those companies have established market power. By the time the union arrives at the table, the platform has millions of users, hundreds of millions in revenue, and enormous leverage. NOIZY's consent-as-code architecture makes the platform unable to exploit without consent — structurally, before any negotiation is required.

**Unions represent the employed, not the independent.** The vast majority of musicians, voice artists, and creators worldwide are not union members, not because they oppose unions, but because they are independent contractors, gig workers, and self-employed artists who work across borders and genres and platforms that unions were never designed to cover.

**Collective bargaining is slow.** The AI revolution is moving in months. Collective bargaining moves in contract cycles measured in years. By the time the next AFM contract is negotiated, the landscape will have shifted three more times. The consent architecture needs to move at the speed of the technology.

### The NOIZY Position: Make Unions Optional, Not Adversarial

NOIZY's answer is not to fight the unions. It is to build a system so good that union membership becomes optional for any artist who joins the Guild.

When an artist has:
- A permanently registered, SHA-256-hashed, legally protected voice
- A 75/25 perpetual royalty split that activates automatically
- A Voice Estate that protects their work for 70 years past death
- A consent architecture that makes exploitation structurally impossible
- A Cultural Archive that preserves their tradition permanently

...they have more protection than any union contract provides. Not because the union failed. Because the platform was built to make exploitation impossible at the infrastructure level.

**The union cannot give you what consent-as-code gives you. It can only extract remediation after the violation already occurred.**

NOIZY prevents the violation from being technically possible.
