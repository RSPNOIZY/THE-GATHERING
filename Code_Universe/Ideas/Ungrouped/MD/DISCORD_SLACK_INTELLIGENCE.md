## DISCORD — THE DEEP VERSION

### What It Actually Is

Discord started as gaming chat in 2015. By 2026 it's the internet's community layer — 200 million registered users, 19 million active servers every single day. Every major AI company, every major music platform, every serious creative community has a Discord server.

Think of it like this: if a movement is happening online, it's happening in Discord first.

### How It's Structured

```
SERVER  =  a community  (like a city)
  ↓
CHANNELS  =  rooms inside the city
  ├── #general  (open conversation)
  ├── #voice-acting  (topic-specific)
  ├── #music-drops  (share your work)
  └── #bot-commands  (where bots live)
  ↓
VOICE CHANNELS  =  live audio rooms
  (anyone in the server can join and talk in real time)
  ↓
STAGE CHANNELS  =  broadcast rooms
  (one person speaks, everyone else listens — like a live event)
  ↓
THREADS  =  focused side conversations
```

## SLACK — THE DEEP VERSION

### What It Actually Is

Slack is the operating system of the professional world. 38 million daily active users, mostly inside companies, studios, agencies, hospitals, and research institutions. Unlike Discord (public communities), Slack workspaces are private — you're invited in by your team.

Think of it like this: if a business decision is being made in real time, it's being made in a Slack channel.

### How It's Structured

```
WORKSPACE  =  a company's private environment
  ↓
CHANNELS  =  topic rooms
  ├── #engineering  (dev team)
  ├── #audio-production  (studio team)
  ├── #legal  (contracts, compliance)
  └── #gabriel-commands  (where GABRIEL lives)
  ↓
THREADS  =  focused replies inside channels
  ↓
DIRECT MESSAGES  =  private conversations
  ↓
APPS/INTEGRATIONS  =  bots and tools wired into the workspace
```
